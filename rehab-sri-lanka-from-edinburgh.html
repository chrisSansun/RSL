Edinburgh, Scotland and addiction treatment

Why Edinburgh residents are choosing Sri Lanka

Edinburgh is one of Europe's most beautiful and culturally rich cities. It is also the capital of a country with one of the highest rates of alcohol-related harm in the developed world. Scotland's alcohol-specific death rate is nearly double that of England and Wales, and Edinburgh, for all its elegance, is not immune. The Festival, the Old Town, the professional world of Holyrood and the financial sector: alcohol is present everywhere and its misuse is rarely discussed honestly.

NHS Scotland provides addiction services through Alcohol and Drug Partnerships, but residential rehabilitation is hard to access. Criteria are strict, funding decisions are slow, and waiting lists mean that someone who is ready to commit to treatment today may not receive a placement for six months to a year. That gap is dangerous.

Scotland's leading private residential rehab providers, including Castle Craig Hospital in Peeblesshire and The Priory's Edinburgh facility, charge between £10,000 and £18,000 for a 28-day residential programme. These are serious facilities, but the cost places them beyond reach for many Edinburgh families.

Sri Lanka offers internationally trained clinicians, evidence-based residential programmes, and a setting that provides complete separation from Edinburgh's social world, at a cost of from $9,900 USD (approx. £7,800) for 28 days. Edinburgh Airport connects to Colombo via London Heathrow or via Dubai with Emirates.

The Scottish context

Why Edinburgh clients benefit from going abroad

Scotland's relationship with alcohol is unique in the UK. So are the barriers that Edinburgh residents face when trying to access serious treatment. Here is what makes Sri Lanka particularly relevant for this market.

Scotland's alcohol problem is not being solved locally

Scotland has legislated for minimum unit pricing and invested in public health campaigns. Alcohol-related deaths have still remained stubbornly high. For individuals in Edinburgh who need intensive residential treatment, the domestic system is not coping with demand. Sri Lanka provides immediate access to the level of care needed.

Edinburgh's tight professional networks

Edinburgh's legal, financial, and political worlds are small and interconnected. Being seen entering a local clinic, or being known to be in treatment, can feel professionally catastrophic. Treatment in Sri Lanka provides the discretion that Edinburgh's professional community often requires as a condition of seeking help.

Climate as a therapeutic factor

Edinburgh's climate is grey, cold, and demanding for much of the year. Sri Lanka's tropical warmth, natural beauty, and unhurried pace provide a physical and psychological shift that supports recovery in ways that are genuinely difficult to replicate in Scotland. Many clients describe the climate as unexpectedly central to their experience.

Cost that makes residential treatment accessible

Scotland's private rehab sector is expensive. Castle Craig and similar high-quality providers charge rates that exclude most families. Sri Lanka brings the same clinical standard within reach. For Edinburgh clients, it is often not a compromise but the only realistic path to proper residential care.

Dual diagnosis and complexity

Scotland has high rates of co-occurring mental health and addiction issues. The clinics we work with in Sri Lanka have significant expertise in dual diagnosis, treating depression, anxiety, trauma, and PTSD alongside substance dependency. This is not a basic detox offering.

Return to Edinburgh with a plan

Clients return to Edinburgh with a structured relapse prevention plan, referrals to local aftercare services, and, where appropriate, an introduction to Scottish support groups. The transition home is prepared, not improvised.

Common questions from Edinburgh clients

Questions we hear from Edinburgh

Is Scotland's alcohol problem really worse than the rest of the UK?
Yes, by a significant margin. Scotland's alcohol-specific death rate is consistently around 80 to 100% higher than England's, even after adjusting for population and deprivation. Edinburgh, as a city, reflects this nationally elevated risk. The reasons are complex and involve culture, history, and the structure of the drinks industry in Scotland, but the public health reality is clear. Acknowledging this is not stigmatising. It is important context for why seeking proper residential treatment is a serious and urgent decision.
How does NHS Scotland compare to NHS England for addiction treatment?
NHS Scotland has made significant investment in addiction services through Alcohol and Drug Partnerships, and in some respects the framework is ahead of England's. However, residential rehabilitation remains rationed, and waiting times for a funded residential placement in Edinburgh are typically six months to a year for those who meet the criteria. Many people who need residential care do not meet the threshold for NHS funding or are assessed as suitable for community treatment only.
What about Castle Craig? Is it not a better option for Scottish clients?
Castle Craig is a well-regarded residential facility and a serious clinical option for those who can afford it. At £10,000 to £18,000 per month, it is out of reach for many Edinburgh families. Sri Lanka's programmes provide a comparable clinical framework and an equally structured residential environment from $9,900 USD per month, with the significant benefit of complete geographical separation from Edinburgh's social world. For many clients, both factors together make Sri Lanka the stronger choice.
Can someone with a complex drinking history travel safely?
Safely, yes, with appropriate planning. A thorough pre-admission assessment is completed before any travel arrangements are confirmed. Where a medically supervised detox is required, this is either completed in the UK prior to travel, or the programme begins with in-facility medical detoxification on arrival. The decision is made on a case-by-case basis and always with client safety as the first priority.
How do I explain an absence from work?
This is a question almost every Edinburgh client raises, particularly those in professional roles. The most common approach is to take leave as medical leave or a personal health matter, without disclosing the specific nature of treatment. Employers are not entitled to know the details of a medical absence. We can advise on how other clients have managed this sensitively, but the decision about what to disclose, to whom, and when, is entirely yours.
