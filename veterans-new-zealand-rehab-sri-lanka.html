Veterans' Affairs New Zealand

What New Zealand veterans need to know about VANZ and overseas treatment

Veterans' Affairs New Zealand (VANZ), administered through the New Zealand Defence Force and the Ministry of Social Development, provides health and rehabilitation entitlements for veterans whose conditions arise from qualifying service. The Veterans' Support Act 2014 is the primary legislation governing these entitlements and establishes the eligibility criteria for treatment funding.

VANZ can fund treatment for physical and mental health conditions - including PTSD, depression, anxiety, and alcohol use disorder - where those conditions are accepted as related to qualifying operational service. Qualifying service includes operational deployments recognised under the Veterans' Support Act, such as service in Vietnam, East Timor, Afghanistan, the Solomon Islands, and a range of other deployments.

For overseas treatment, VANZ requires prior approval through a treatment authority process. This involves a referral from a treating GP, a clinical assessment, and a formal application to VANZ establishing that the proposed treatment is clinically appropriate and that equivalent treatment is not reasonably accessible domestically. We assist veterans in preparing this documentation and can provide clinical rationale to support an overseas treatment application.

For veterans who do not have VANZ-accepted conditions, or who prefer to move faster than the approval process allows, self-funded treatment in Sri Lanka is an accessible alternative. New Zealand is well-connected to Colombo - a single connection via Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, or Melbourne brings total travel time to around 12 to 14 hours from Auckland.

Why New Zealand veterans choose overseas treatment

What Sri Lanka offers New Zealand veterans that domestic services cannot

New Zealand's veteran mental health and addiction services are improving, but the combination of geography, small population, and limited specialist residential capacity creates real barriers for veterans who need intensive residential treatment quickly.

Limited specialist residential capacity

New Zealand has a small number of specialist residential programmes for veterans with PTSD and co-occurring addiction. Waiting times at the few that exist are significant, and integrated trauma and addiction treatment in a residential setting remains difficult to access domestically.

A close and practical destination

Sri Lanka is one of the most accessible overseas destinations from New Zealand for extended treatment. A single connection from Auckland brings total travel time to around 12 to 14 hours - comparable to flying within the Pacific region. Sri Lanka Airlines, Singapore Airlines, and Air New Zealand codeshares serve this route.

Cost significantly below New Zealand private care

Private residential addiction treatment in New Zealand at a reputable facility costs between NZD $18,000 and NZD $35,000 for 28 days. Sri Lanka starts from $9,900 USD (approximately NZD $16,000) per month - making a 60 to 90-day programme affordable where a 28-day domestic programme would exhaust most budgets.

Integrated PTSD and addiction treatment

The programmes we work with treat operational trauma and substance use disorder as a single clinical presentation - not as sequential referrals to separate services. This integrated approach is what veterans with co-occurring PTSD and addiction require and what domestic services in New Zealand often cannot provide within a single residential placement.

Complete separation from NZ life

Geographic separation from New Zealand's close-knit communities, veteran networks, and the environments where addiction developed is consistently reported by veterans as one of the most clinically significant aspects of overseas treatment. The privacy of being unknown in a different country removes social pressures that domestic treatment cannot.

Privacy and confidentiality

Many New Zealand veterans, particularly from smaller communities or with ongoing NZDF connections, are concerned about being recognised in domestic treatment settings. Private overseas treatment operates completely outside the NZDF and VANZ systems unless you choose to provide records, and treatment is fully confidential.

The process for NZ veterans

From first contact to admission in Sri Lanka

Whether pursuing VANZ funding or self-funding, we manage the process around you so you can focus on the decision to get well.

1

Confirm your VANZ eligibility

Contact VANZ on 0800 483 8372 to confirm your qualifying service status and whether your conditions have been accepted under the Veterans' Support Act. If you have not previously applied to VANZ for health treatment, a VANZ case manager can guide you through the initial assessment.

2

Contact us for a free consultation

We discuss your clinical presentation, your service background, and what the right programme looks like for you. We advise on the VANZ process where applicable, and can move to self-funded admission immediately for veterans who prefer to proceed without VANZ involvement.

3

Treatment authority application (if VANZ-funded)

We prepare supporting documentation for a VANZ treatment authority application - including clinical rationale, provider credentials, and evidence of why overseas treatment is appropriate. Your GP provides the formal referral. VANZ assesses the application and, if approved, authorises funding.

4

Travel from New Zealand

Flights from Auckland to Colombo connect through Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, or Melbourne. Total travel time is approximately 12 to 14 hours. You are met at Colombo airport and accompanied to the facility. The first days include comprehensive clinical assessment and individualised treatment planning.

Common questions

Questions from New Zealand veterans and their families

I served in East Timor and Afghanistan. Am I eligible for VANZ funding?
East Timor and Afghanistan are both recognised qualifying service deployments under the Veterans' Support Act 2014. If you have a health condition - including PTSD, depression, or alcohol use disorder - that arose from or was significantly contributed to by that service, you may be eligible for VANZ-funded treatment. Contact VANZ on 0800 483 8372 to confirm your status and initiate an eligibility assessment.
Will VANZ fund treatment in Sri Lanka?
VANZ can fund overseas treatment subject to prior approval through a treatment authority process. The key requirements are that the treatment is clinically appropriate, that the overseas provider is a qualified facility, and that equivalent treatment is not readily accessible domestically. We can prepare documentation to support this application. For veterans who prefer to self-fund, no VANZ approval is required.
How does VANZ treatment funding interact with ACC?
ACC (Accident Compensation Corporation) covers the costs of treatment for physical injuries caused by accidents - including accidents during military service. VANZ covers health conditions arising from qualifying service that are not covered by ACC. The two systems are separate, and entitlements under one do not generally affect the other. If you have both an ACC claim for a physical injury and a VANZ claim for a mental health condition, both can be active simultaneously.
Can my family stay in contact with me during treatment?
Yes. The time difference between New Zealand and Sri Lanka is approximately 6.5 hours (NZST in summer) - Sri Lanka is behind New Zealand. Evening calls from New Zealand family members typically fall during Sri Lanka daytime hours. The clinic will discuss communication policies at the start of treatment; most programmes encourage limited contact in the first week to support the adjustment to residential care.
What if my VANZ application is declined?
VANZ decisions can be reviewed and appealed. If an initial application is declined, we can assist with a stronger supporting submission on re-application. In the meantime, self-funded treatment is available and can be arranged within 5 to 10 days. Many New Zealand veterans choose to self-fund for speed and privacy, and pursue a VANZ reimbursement application retrospectively where the eligibility criteria are met.
What support is available when I return to New Zealand?
Aftercare planning is built into the treatment programme. On return to New Zealand, this typically involves re-engagement with VANZ for ongoing health support, referral to a community mental health service or GP for continuing therapy, and participation in a relapse prevention plan developed during treatment. The New Zealand Defence Force also operates a range of veteran welfare services, and organisations such as the RSA and Veterans NZ provide community-based peer support.
