The VA Foreign Medical Program

What US veterans need to know about overseas treatment

The VA Foreign Medical Program (FMP) is a VA benefit that provides reimbursement for medical treatment received outside the United States. It is designed for veterans who are living abroad or travelling internationally and need treatment for a service-connected condition - that is, a condition the VA has formally recognised as connected to their military service.

PTSD and substance use disorder are among the most common service-connected conditions. For veterans with a VA service-connection rating for PTSD - including those where alcohol or substance use disorder is rated as secondary to PTSD - FMP can provide a pathway to reimbursement for residential treatment received in Sri Lanka.

FMP works on a reimbursement basis, not pre-payment. The veteran pays for treatment upfront and submits a claim to the VA for reimbursement at US Medicare rates. The difference between the Sri Lankan cost and the Medicare reimbursement rate varies by case, but given that private residential treatment in Sri Lanka starts from $9,900 USD per month compared to $30,000 to $60,000 in the United States, the economics often still favour overseas treatment even accounting for the reimbursement cap.

For veterans who do not have a service-connected PTSD or SUD rating, or who want to move faster than the FMP process allows, self-funded treatment is an equally viable option. Many US veterans choose to pay privately for treatment in Sri Lanka given the significant cost differential compared to domestic private residential care.

Understanding your coverage

What VA FMP covers - and what it does not

FMP has specific rules about what is and is not covered. Understanding these before you seek treatment is essential to avoid unexpected out-of-pocket costs.

Covered

PTSD - service-connected

Residential treatment for PTSD where PTSD carries a VA service-connection rating is reimbursable under FMP. This includes trauma-focused therapy, EMDR, and prolonged exposure delivered in an inpatient residential setting.

Covered

Substance use disorder - secondary to PTSD

Where the VA has rated alcohol use disorder or substance use disorder as secondary to a service-connected condition such as PTSD, FMP can cover treatment for the substance use condition within an integrated residential programme.

Covered

Mental health conditions - service-connected

Depression, anxiety, and other mental health conditions that carry a VA service-connection rating are covered under FMP when treated by a qualified provider in a residential or outpatient clinical setting.

Not covered under FMP

Non-service-connected conditions

FMP does not cover treatment for conditions that have not been formally rated as service-connected by the VA. If your substance use disorder does not carry a rating, FMP will not reimburse treatment for it as a standalone condition.

Not covered under FMP

Treatment before FMP registration

Veterans must register with the VA FMP programme before seeking treatment overseas. Claims for treatment received before registration are generally not reimbursed. Registration is free and can be completed before travelling.

Consider self-funding

Faster admission without FMP

FMP is a post-treatment reimbursement programme, not an approval or pre-authorisation system. Veterans who need to move quickly - or who do not have a service-connected rating - can access treatment self-funded and begin within 5 to 10 days.

How to use VA FMP for overseas treatment

The process from registration to reimbursement

FMP is a straightforward reimbursement process once you understand the steps. Here is how it works in practice for a veteran seeking residential treatment in Sri Lanka.

1

Confirm your service-connection rating

Before registering for FMP, confirm which conditions carry a VA service-connection rating. You will find this on your most recent VA Rating Decision letter or through your eBenefits account. PTSD, depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders rated as secondary to a service-connected condition are all potentially covered.

2

Register with VA FMP

Contact the VA Foreign Medical Program directly by phone at 1-877-345-8179 or by completing VA Form 10-7959f-1. Registration is free and confirms your enrolled status. This step must be completed before you seek treatment overseas.

3

Arrange treatment in Sri Lanka

Contact us to discuss your clinical presentation and the right programme. We assist with clinical matching, admission logistics, and can provide the documentation FMP requires for a reimbursement claim - including provider credentials and itemised treatment records.

4

Submit your FMP claim

After treatment, you submit a claim to VA FMP using Form 10-7959f-2, along with itemised invoices and your treatment records. Reimbursement is calculated at US Medicare rates for equivalent services. Claims are processed by the FMP contractor and payment is made directly to you.

Why US veterans choose Sri Lanka

What Sri Lanka offers that VA facilities and US private clinics cannot

VA facilities have improved significantly, but they carry inherent limitations - waiting lists, institutional culture, and a clinical environment that many veterans find inhibits recovery. Here is what overseas private treatment provides instead.

No VA waiting lists

VA residential mental health and addiction programmes operate waiting lists that can run to weeks or months. For a veteran in crisis, waiting is not a clinical option. Private admission to Sri Lanka can be arranged in 5 to 10 days from initial contact.

Outside the VA system

Treatment in a private overseas clinic operates completely independently of the VA. Records are not shared with the VA without your explicit consent, and the therapeutic environment is civilian, private, and free of military institutional dynamics.

Integrated PTSD and addiction treatment

The programmes we work with treat PTSD and substance use disorder as a single integrated clinical presentation - not as separate conditions treated by separate services. This is the clinical approach that evidence supports and that VA outpatient services often struggle to deliver in an integrated way.

Cost - a fraction of US private care

US private residential addiction treatment at a reputable facility costs between $30,000 and $60,000 for 28 days. Sri Lanka starts from $9,900 USD per month. For veterans funding treatment privately, this difference is significant enough to enable a 60 or 90-day programme at the same cost as 28 days in the US.

Complete geographic remove

Recovery from operational trauma and addiction is harder in the environments where both developed. Sri Lanka provides complete geographic separation from home, work, social networks, and the triggers embedded in everyday American life.

Confidentiality and security clearances

Many veterans with active or former security clearances are concerned about how a formal mental health diagnosis in a VA or domestic setting might affect their clearance status. Treatment records from a private overseas clinic are not reported to any US government agency.

Common questions from US veterans

Questions we hear from US veterans and their families

Do I need to register with VA FMP before seeking treatment in Sri Lanka?
Yes, if you intend to submit a reimbursement claim through FMP. Registration must be completed before treatment begins. It is free and can be done by phone at 1-877-345-8179 or by submitting VA Form 10-7959f-1. If you are self-funding treatment, FMP registration is not required and you can arrange admission within days.
My PTSD is service-connected but my alcohol use disorder is not rated separately. Can I still claim?
Potentially yes. Where alcohol use disorder or substance use disorder is secondary to a service-connected condition - meaning it developed as a result of or in connection with PTSD - the VA can rate the substance use disorder as secondary to PTSD. If this secondary rating is established, FMP can cover treatment for both conditions within an integrated residential programme. Speak to a Veterans Service Officer (VSO) about establishing a secondary rating if you have not already done so.
Will treatment in Sri Lanka affect my VA disability rating?
No. Treatment records from a private overseas clinic are not automatically submitted to the VA. Your records are private and confidential. Nothing from your treatment in Sri Lanka will affect your VA rating unless you choose to submit those records yourself - for example, as supporting evidence in a future claim or appeal.
How much will VA FMP actually reimburse?
FMP reimburses at US Medicare rates for equivalent services. Because Sri Lanka's costs are significantly lower than US domestic rates, the reimbursement may partially or fully cover the cost of treatment depending on the specific services provided and how they are billed. We assist with itemised documentation to maximise the accuracy of your claim. For most veterans, even a partial reimbursement makes overseas treatment more cost-effective than US domestic private care.
What is the flight from the US to Sri Lanka like?
From the US West Coast, travel to Colombo is typically 18 to 20 hours via a single connection - commonly through Dubai, Doha, or Singapore. From the East Coast, add 2 to 3 hours. Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Singapore Airlines all serve Colombo with comfortable business and economy options. The journey is long but straightforward, and the clinical team can advise on managing the travel in terms of medical safety during the journey.
What if I need to come home before my programme ends?
Discharge before programme completion is always the veteran's choice. We recommend flexible return flights and comprehensive travel insurance for all clients. The clinical team will discuss the risks of early departure and will work with you on a safe transition plan if early return becomes necessary.
