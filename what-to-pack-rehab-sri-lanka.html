What to Pack for a 28-Day Rehab Stay in Sri Lanka

Packing for a month in residential treatment is different from packing for a holiday. This guide covers what you actually need - and what you should leave at home.

5 minute read · Updated 2026

What the clinic provides

Before listing what to bring, it is worth establishing what you do not need to bring. Quality residential facilities in Sri Lanka provide the practical infrastructure of daily life - bedding, towels, meals, laundry service, and in most cases basic toiletries. You are not packing for camping or for a place that has forgotten to prepare for your arrival.

The list of what is typically provided includes:

  • All meals and snacks throughout the day
  • Bed linen and towels (changed regularly)
  • Laundry service, usually several times a week
  • Basic toiletries as a starter pack (soap, shampoo, toothpaste)
  • Wi-Fi access, within the programme's guidelines on device use
  • All clinical materials, journals, and workbooks used in the programme

The upshot is that you can travel lighter than you might expect. A single medium-sized suitcase and a carry-on bag is usually more than enough for 28 days. The instinct to over-pack - perhaps reflecting a desire to feel prepared for an uncertain experience - is understandable, but unnecessary.

Clothing - climate considerations

Sri Lanka is tropical. Even the Hill Country, which is cooler than the coasts, is significantly warmer than most of the destinations international patients are travelling from. The practical implication is that your wardrobe for treatment should be built around light, breathable fabrics - cotton in particular.

What to bring for clothing:

  • Light cotton t-shirts and tops: Enough for every day - roughly 10 to 14. These will be washed regularly, but having a comfortable supply matters when you are focused on other things.
  • Lightweight trousers or shorts: 4 to 6 pairs. Loose-fitting and comfortable. Jeans are possible but not ideal in the heat - one pair maximum.
  • A light layer for evenings: In the Hill Country especially, evenings can be genuinely cool. One light cardigan or long-sleeved top is useful year-round.
  • Comfortable shoes: Trainers or walking shoes for exercise sessions and group walks. Sandals or flip-flops for around the facility. You do not need formal footwear.
  • Swimwear: If the facility has a pool - which many do - this is worth packing.
  • Comfortable sleepwear: Light cotton. Sleep quality in early recovery is often disrupted; comfortable nightwear is a small thing that helps.

What to leave behind: formal or business clothing, heavy fabrics, multiple pairs of shoes, anything you would be upset about losing or damaging. Treatment is not an occasion that requires dressing up.

Medications and prescriptions

This section requires specific attention. If you take prescribed medications - for any condition, not only addiction-related - you must bring an adequate supply for the full duration of your stay, plus a reasonable buffer. Bring the original pharmacy packaging where possible, along with a letter from your prescribing doctor confirming the medication, dosage, and your identity.

Do not assume that your prescription medication will be readily available in Sri Lanka. Some medications are available; many are not, or are available only in different formulations. Bring enough.

You should disclose all medications to the admissions team before arrival - not after. The clinical team needs this information to manage your care safely, particularly during any detox process where drug interactions can have serious consequences. This disclosure is confidential and will not be used to judge you.

Electronics and phones

Most residential programmes in Sri Lanka allow patients to retain their phones, subject to guidelines about when and how they can be used. The first week or two often involves restricted phone access - not as a punitive measure, but because constant contact with the home environment can significantly undermine the early recovery process.

Useful electronics to bring:

  • Your smartphone (check with the facility about its policy before assuming full access)
  • Headphones - useful for music, guided meditations, and downtime
  • A Kindle or e-reader, if you are a reader - books are genuinely useful in treatment
  • A travel adapter (Sri Lanka uses Type D and Type G sockets)
  • A portable charger

What to consider leaving at home: laptops, unless there is a genuine work need that has been discussed with the facility in advance. Work-related computing is one of the most common ways people avoid engaging fully with treatment, and most programmes actively discourage it during the first weeks.

Personal care

Beyond the basics that the facility provides, personal care items worth packing include:

  • Your preferred toiletries - shampoo, conditioner, skincare products you use regularly
  • High-factor sunscreen (SPF 50). The tropical sun is intense and the outdoor component of the programme means real exposure.
  • Insect repellent - particularly for evenings in coastal or garden settings
  • A small first aid kit with any over-the-counter items you rely on (antihistamines, antacids, paracetamol)
  • A journal and pens - journalling is a core part of most residential programmes and having your own notebook matters to many people

What not to bring

The following items are typically prohibited or strongly discouraged in residential treatment facilities:

  • Alcohol, recreational drugs, or any substance not declared on admission - this is an absolute prohibition and a serious one
  • Prescription medications not disclosed to the clinical team
  • Mouthwash or products containing alcohol (alcohol-free alternatives are available)
  • Aerosols or products with solvents
  • Expensive jewellery or items of significant financial or sentimental value that you cannot afford to lose
  • Large amounts of cash - most needs are covered within the programme fee

If you are unsure whether something is permitted, ask before you travel. The admissions team will give you a clear answer. No reasonable question about what to bring will be judged - the goal is for you to arrive as prepared and as settled as possible.

Documents to carry

Travel and administrative documents to pack:

  • Passport (valid for at least six months beyond your return date)
  • ETA confirmation printout or accessible on your phone
  • Travel insurance documents, including the emergency contact number
  • Details of your travel insurance policy, specifically coverage for medical treatment abroad
  • Any pre-admission paperwork or correspondence from the facility
  • Emergency contact details for family members
  • Prescriptions and medical letters as noted above

The mindset about possessions

One thing that consistently surprises people about residential treatment is how quickly the question of possessions becomes irrelevant. Within a few days of arrival, what you packed matters very little. The days are full, the work is demanding, and the texture of the experience has very little to do with whether you brought the right moisturiser.

The act of packing well is useful - it is a practical task that you can complete before departure, when other aspects of the decision may feel less controllable. But it is worth keeping in perspective. The most important thing you are bringing to treatment is not in your suitcase.
