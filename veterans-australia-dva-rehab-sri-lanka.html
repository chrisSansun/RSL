DVA and mental health treatment

What Australian veterans need to know about DVA funding

The Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA) administers a range of health and rehabilitation benefits for Australian veterans, ADF members, and their families. For veterans dealing with mental health conditions and addiction, two pathways are particularly important: the Non-Liability Health Care (NLHC) program, and treatment entitlements under the Gold Card and White Card schemes.

The NLHC program is a significant and often underused entitlement. Under NLHC, eligible veterans can receive funded treatment for specific mental health conditions — including PTSD, depression, anxiety, and alcohol use disorder — without needing to establish that those conditions are related to their service. This makes NLHC one of the most accessible mental health funding pathways available to Australian veterans, and one of the most relevant for veterans seeking residential treatment for addiction.

For overseas treatment, DVA generally requires prior approval through a Treatment Notification or Treatment Authority process. We assist veterans in preparing the documentation required and liaising with DVA through an approved referrer where applicable. For veterans who prefer to move more quickly, or where DVA approval is uncertain, self-funded treatment in Sri Lanka remains an accessible and cost-effective alternative — at $9,900 USD per month compared to $25,000 to $40,000 AUD for equivalent private residential treatment in Australia.

Sri Lanka is approximately 9 to 11 hours from the Australian east coast — a shorter journey than flying to many European or US destinations, and direct services operate from major Australian cities.

Understanding your DVA card

Gold Card, White Card, and Non-Liability Health Care

Your DVA entitlements depend on your card type and eligibility. Here is what each card covers — and how Non-Liability Health Care extends access to mental health treatment beyond what card type alone provides.

Non-Liability Health Care — the most important entitlement for veterans with mental health conditions

Under DVA's Non-Liability Health Care program, eligible veterans can access funded treatment for PTSD, depression, anxiety, and alcohol use disorder without needing to prove those conditions were caused by their service. This is a critical distinction: you do not need to have a formal service-related disability acceptance for these conditions to access NLHC treatment funding. Eligibility is based on eligible service, not on a diagnosis linked to that service. Many veterans who believe they are not entitled to DVA funding for mental health treatment are, in fact, eligible under NLHC.

Gold Card

Repatriation Health Card — For All Conditions

The Gold Card provides the broadest DVA health coverage, funding treatment for all health conditions — not just those related to service. Gold Card holders are generally eligible for a wide range of inpatient, outpatient, and community health services. For Gold Card holders, residential mental health and addiction treatment can be funded by DVA subject to treatment authority from a recognised DVA provider.

White Card

Repatriation Health Card — For Specific Conditions

The White Card covers treatment for specific conditions that have been accepted as related to service — for example, a physical injury sustained in training, or a mental health condition formally linked to operational service. White Card holders can also access Non-Liability Health Care for the specific mental health conditions covered under NLHC, regardless of whether those conditions appear on their White Card.

Accessing DVA-funded overseas treatment

The process for Australian veterans seeking treatment in Sri Lanka

The process for accessing DVA-funded overseas treatment involves several steps. We assist with this throughout — from initial eligibility check through to arrival at the clinic.

1

Confirm your DVA eligibility

Contact DVA on 1800 555 254 to confirm your card status and NLHC eligibility. If you are unsure of your entitlements, a Veterans' Welfare Officer through RSL Australia, DVA, or the ADF can assist with a review of your current entitlements at no cost.

2

Obtain a referral and DVA Treatment Authority

For funded overseas treatment, DVA requires a referral from a treating doctor and a Treatment Authority — a formal approval from DVA for the specific treatment at the specific provider. We assist with the documentation needed to support this application, including clinical rationale for overseas treatment.

3

Clinical assessment and programme matching

Contact us to discuss your situation and clinical needs. We match you to the right programme — one that has experience with military trauma and co-occurring addiction — and provide the clinical information DVA and your GP will need to support the Treatment Authority application.

4

Travel and admission

Flights from Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, and Perth to Colombo are available with one connection, typically via Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, or Dubai. Journey time from the east coast is approximately 9 to 11 hours. Airport transfer to the facility is arranged by the clinic.

Why Australian veterans choose Sri Lanka

What overseas private treatment offers that DVA-approved domestic services cannot

DVA-funded services in Australia have improved, but Australian veterans seeking residential addiction and trauma treatment still face significant barriers. Here is what Sri Lanka provides instead.

Faster admission than domestic DVA pathways

DVA approval processes and waiting lists at Australian inpatient facilities can extend treatment access by weeks or months. For self-funded cases in Sri Lanka, admission can be arranged in 5 to 10 days. Even for DVA-funded cases, the overseas option is often faster than a domestic residential bed.

Integrated PTSD and addiction treatment

Many Australian residential services treat addiction and PTSD as separate streams. The programmes we work with treat them as a single clinical presentation — which is what the evidence supports and what the majority of veterans actually need.

Complete separation from Australian life

Veterans consistently report that geographic distance is one of the most important clinical factors in their recovery. Sri Lanka is close enough to reach easily from Australia but far enough to provide genuine separation from triggers, social networks, and the environments where addiction developed.

Cost significantly below Australian private care

Private residential treatment in Australia at a reputable facility costs between $25,000 and $40,000 AUD for 28 days. Sri Lanka starts from $9,900 USD (approximately $15,000 AUD) per month — enabling longer stays and more intensive treatment for the same budget.

Privacy outside the DVA and ADF system

Some veterans are concerned about how a formal DVA mental health claim might affect their career, security clearances, or standing within veteran communities. Private overseas treatment operates completely outside the DVA system, with no automatic reporting to the ADF or Australian government.

Open Arms as a support pathway

Open Arms — Veterans & Families Counselling — provides free counselling to current and former ADF members and their families. We work collaboratively with Open Arms counsellors and encourage veterans to engage with Open Arms both before and after residential treatment as part of their ongoing care plan.

Common questions from Australian veterans

Questions we hear from Australian veterans and their families

I have a White Card but my alcohol use disorder is not listed as an accepted condition. Can DVA still fund treatment?
Potentially yes. Under DVA's Non-Liability Health Care program, alcohol use disorder is one of the specific conditions that can be funded without a service-related acceptance — provided you meet the basic eligibility criteria based on your service history. This is one of the most frequently misunderstood aspects of DVA entitlements. Contact DVA on 1800 555 254 or speak to a Veterans' Welfare Officer to confirm your NLHC eligibility.
Does DVA need to approve my treatment before I travel to Sri Lanka?
Yes, for DVA-funded treatment. DVA requires a Treatment Authority for funded care, particularly for overseas providers. This involves a referral from your GP or treating medical officer and an application to DVA for approval. We assist with the documentation to support this process. For self-funded treatment, no DVA approval is required and you can arrange admission directly within days.
Will making a DVA claim affect my compensation entitlements or MRCA/DRCA claims?
Accessing DVA health treatment does not automatically affect your compensation entitlements under the MRCA or DRCA. However, if you are in the process of making or appealing a compensation claim, it is worth discussing the timing with a Veterans' Welfare Officer or legal adviser before accessing treatment. Records from treatment can, with your consent, be used as supporting evidence in compensation claims.
What is the flight from Australia to Sri Lanka like?
Flights from Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane to Colombo connect through Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, or Dubai. Total travel time from the east coast is approximately 9 to 11 hours. From Perth, it is slightly shorter — around 7 to 8 hours. Singapore Airlines, Malaysia Airlines, Emirates, and Qatar Airways all serve Colombo from Australian cities. It is a manageable journey and significantly shorter than flying to the UK or US.
Can my family contact me during treatment?
Yes. The time difference between Sri Lanka and Australia (AEST) is approximately 4.5 hours — Sri Lanka is behind Australia. This means morning calls from Australian family members are easy to schedule during Sri Lankan evening hours. The clinic will advise on communication policies at the start of treatment; many programmes encourage limited phone contact in the first week to support the transition into treatment.
What support is available after I return to Australia?
Aftercare planning is built into the treatment programme. This typically includes a structured transition back to Australian life, referrals to continuing care services, and a relapse prevention plan. Open Arms — Veterans & Families Counselling — provides ongoing free counselling for ADF veterans and is often the primary continuing care provider after residential treatment. We remain available for guidance and support after discharge.
