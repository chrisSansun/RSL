UK veterans and addiction treatment

Why so many UK veterans look beyond the NHS for residential treatment

The NHS has made genuine progress in veterans' mental health through Op COURAGE - the Veterans Mental Health and Wellbeing Service - which provides assessment, treatment, and referral for serving personnel, veterans, and their families. Op COURAGE can be a valuable starting point, particularly for mental health assessment and outpatient therapy for PTSD and depression.

Where Op COURAGE falls short is in the provision of residential addiction treatment. The NHS does not routinely fund residential rehabilitation for alcohol or drug addiction, and when it does, waiting times are substantial - often 12 to 18 months for a specialist residential bed. Many veterans with co-occurring PTSD and substance use disorder find that the system treats these as separate problems: mental health referred one way, addiction referred another, with no integrated residential programme available.

UK veterans have several routes available beyond the NHS. The charitable sector provides some of the most credible alternatives - Combat Stress, SSAFA, The Royal British Legion, and a range of regimental charities all provide funding assistance to varying degrees. For veterans who have received AFCS (Armed Forces Compensation Scheme) payments or who have access to savings, self-funded private residential treatment is also a realistic option - particularly overseas, where cost is significantly lower than private UK clinics.

A 28-day private residential programme at a UK clinic such as Priory, UKAT, or Clouds House costs between £10,000 and £18,000. The equivalent standard of care in Sri Lanka starts from $9,900 USD (approximately £7,800) per month - with a direct 10.5-hour flight from Heathrow.

Understanding your NHS entitlements

Op COURAGE, NHS addiction services, and what you are actually entitled to

The NHS provides more for veterans than many realise - but also significantly less than many need when it comes to residential addiction treatment. Here is an honest breakdown.

Op COURAGE - what it covers

Op COURAGE (Veterans Mental Health and Wellbeing Service) provides free assessment, therapy, and referral for veterans with PTSD, depression, anxiety, and other mental health conditions. It includes access to trauma-focused CBT, EMDR, and peer support. Op COURAGE is accessed via GP referral or self-referral.

Op COURAGE - what it does not cover

Op COURAGE does not provide residential addiction treatment. It can refer to NHS Drug and Alcohol services (DAAT/DAT), but residential detox and rehabilitation through the NHS is rationed, subject to local authority funding, and typically involves waiting lists of 6 to 18 months for veterans in most areas.

NHS residential rehab - the reality

NHS funding for residential addiction rehabilitation is not an automatic entitlement. It requires a local authority funding panel decision, is subject to a residence-based needs assessment, and is often restricted to the most severe presentations. Most veterans with alcohol dependency do not meet the threshold for an NHS-funded residential bed, even with co-occurring PTSD.

Combat Stress - specialist PTSD treatment

Combat Stress provides residential treatment for veterans with PTSD, including a programme at its residential centres in Surrey, Scotland, and Wales. Treatment is free for eligible veterans and covers trauma-focused therapy and co-occurring mental health conditions. Combat Stress does not primarily treat substance use disorders but can treat veterans where PTSD is the primary diagnosis.

Integrated PTSD and addiction - the gap

The most significant gap in UK veterans' provision is integrated residential treatment for PTSD and addiction together. Combat Stress focuses on PTSD. NHS addiction services focus on substance use. For veterans who have both - which is the majority - neither route provides truly integrated care within a residential setting.

Why private overseas fills this gap

Sri Lanka's residential programmes treat PTSD and addiction as a single integrated clinical presentation. There are no separate referral pathways, no waiting lists between services, and no artificial division between the trauma work and the addiction work. This is what the evidence supports - and what the NHS cannot currently provide.

Charity funding routes

Veterans' charities that can help fund treatment

Several UK veterans' charities provide grants or financial assistance that can partially or fully fund private residential treatment - including overseas treatment. Most require evidence of service and a statement of need.

SSAFA - Soldiers, Sailors, Airmen and Families Association

SSAFA Financial Assistance

SSAFA provides caseworker-led financial assistance to serving and former members of the armed forces and their families. SSAFA can provide grants towards the cost of private medical treatment, including residential rehabilitation, where statutory provision is unavailable or inadequate. Applications are assessed case-by-case by trained SSAFA caseworkers. Contact SSAFA on 0800 731 4880.

The Royal British Legion

RBL Welfare and Financial Support

The Royal British Legion provides welfare support and financial assistance to veterans and their families where there is a demonstrable need. RBL does not have a specific rehabilitation grant programme but can provide support through its general welfare fund for veterans facing hardship or medical costs. Contact the RBL on 0808 802 8080.

ABF The Soldiers' Charity

ABF Grants Programme

ABF The Soldiers' Charity provides financial assistance to soldiers, veterans, and their immediate families who are in need. ABF grants are available for a range of purposes including medical treatment and rehabilitation costs. Applications are generally made through SSAFA or RBL caseworkers rather than directly.

Regimental and Corps Charities

Your Regimental Association

Most regiments and corps maintain a welfare charity that provides financial assistance to former members. These associations often move faster and with less bureaucracy than the larger national charities. Your regimental association is worth contacting early in the process. The Veterans' Gateway (0808 802 1212) can help identify the right association if you are unsure.

Common questions from UK veterans

Questions we hear from UK veterans and their families

Can I get NHS funding for treatment in Sri Lanka?
NHS overseas treatment funding (Individual Funding Requests or prior approval for overseas care) is theoretically available but extremely difficult to obtain for residential addiction treatment. The criteria are narrow - generally requiring that equivalent treatment is unavailable in the NHS and that there is evidence of clinical exceptionality. For most UK veterans, NHS overseas funding is not a realistic route. Charity funding or self-funding are more viable options.
I have an AFCS lump sum payment. Can I use this to fund treatment?
Yes. AFCS lump sum payments are yours to use as you choose - there is no restriction on using compensation payments for private medical treatment, including overseas rehabilitation. Many UK veterans use AFCS payments to fund a level of private care that would otherwise be unaffordable. Given that Sri Lanka costs significantly less than equivalent UK private care, an AFCS payment can fund a substantially longer and more intensive programme overseas.
Will accessing private treatment affect my Op COURAGE or NHS referral?
No. Private treatment does not affect your existing NHS referrals or Op COURAGE access. You can have both running concurrently, and records from private treatment are not automatically shared with the NHS. Many veterans access private overseas treatment while keeping their Op COURAGE referral or NHS key working in place as a continuing care resource on return.
How do I apply for SSAFA financial assistance for overseas treatment?
Contact SSAFA on 0800 731 4880 and request a caseworker referral. The caseworker will conduct an assessment of your circumstances and need, and will advise on what financial support SSAFA can provide. We can provide supporting documentation for your application - including evidence of the cost of overseas treatment versus domestic options, and clinical justification for the overseas placement. This documentation typically strengthens the application considerably.
Is the clinical standard in Sri Lanka comparable to UK private clinics?
Yes. The clinical teams we work with are internationally trained, many having studied or worked in the UK, Australia, and the United States. Treatment protocols follow the same evidence-based frameworks used in British private clinics - CBT, EMDR, prolonged exposure, 12-step facilitation, motivational interviewing, and trauma-informed care. The key differences are cost, environment, and geographic separation from the UK. Clinical quality is not a compromise.
What aftercare is available when I return to the UK?
Aftercare planning begins during treatment, not at discharge. On return to the UK, veterans typically re-engage with Op COURAGE for continuing mental health support, establish a local key worker through NHS community drug and alcohol services if ongoing support is needed, and follow a relapse prevention plan developed during treatment. We remain available as a point of contact after discharge, and can advise on UK-based peer support organisations including Veterans with PTSD and Veterans in Recovery.
