Is Sri Lanka safe for rehab?

It is a fair question and you deserve an honest answer, not a sales pitch. Here is the full picture.

The honest answer

Sri Lanka: what you need to know

Sri Lanka went through a serious economic and political crisis in 2022 that received significant international media coverage. It is worth addressing this directly: the crisis was real, it was difficult for Sri Lankans, and it is now largely resolved. The elected government stabilised the economy with IMF support, fuel and medicine shortages ended, and the country has returned to normal function. International tourism, including medical tourism, has recovered strongly. The Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) and equivalent bodies in Australia and the USA do not currently advise against travel to Sri Lanka for most visitors.

For someone considering residential treatment in 2025, Sri Lanka is a stable, safe, and welcoming country. The context of the 2022 crisis is worth knowing, but it is not a reason to reconsider treatment there.

Healthcare standards in Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka has a dual healthcare system: an extensive public health network and a well-developed private sector. The private hospitals in Colombo are genuinely high quality by international standards. Lanka Hospitals, Asiri Hospital Group, and Apollo Hospital Colombo all operate to standards comparable with private healthcare in the UK, Australia, or the Gulf. Their medical staff include doctors who trained in the UK, Australia, the United States, and India. English is widely spoken throughout the private healthcare system.

The rehabilitation clinics we work with operate within this private healthcare infrastructure. Medical oversight is provided by qualified physicians. There are clear protocols for emergency transfers to Colombo's private hospitals in the very unlikely event that this is required.

Safety within the treatment facility

The environment of a residential treatment facility is, by its nature, a supervised and structured one. Clients are in a closed setting with clinical staff present around the clock. There is no access to alcohol or substances. Days are structured and purposeful. This is one of the safest environments in which a person in early recovery can find themselves, arguably safer in many respects than returning home to an unsupported environment.

Facilities have clear protocols for medical emergencies, mental health crises, and any other situation that may arise. Staff are trained in first aid and emergency response. Transfer to a private hospital in Colombo, if required, can typically be arranged within 30 to 60 minutes.

Travel insurance

Travel insurance is essential and non-negotiable. For a 28 to 90 day trip for medical treatment purposes, a comprehensive policy typically costs between £100 and £300 depending on your age, health history, and departure country. The policy should include medical evacuation cover, hospital in-patient treatment, and ideally coverage for treatment-related complications.

Some standard travel insurance policies exclude pre-existing medical conditions or treatment-related travel. Read the policy carefully and speak to the insurer about the purpose of your travel. Specialist medical travel insurance policies are available that provide more comprehensive cover for this kind of trip.

Practical safety considerations

English is widely spoken in Sri Lanka, particularly in the private healthcare and tourism sectors. You will not face a language barrier in any meaningful context during your treatment. The general safety environment for visitors to Sri Lanka is good: the country has low levels of violent crime and is rated as one of the safer countries in Asia for international visitors.

The UK, Australian, American, and European Union embassies all have permanent missions in Colombo. In the event of any diplomatic issue or consular requirement, embassy support is readily available.

A note on expectations

Sri Lanka is a developing country. This means that infrastructure, consumer services, and general amenities outside the private sector may not be to the standard of the UK, Australia, or the UAE. Inside the treatment facility, the standard will be high. But it is worth being honest with yourself about adjusting expectations for what surrounds it. For most clients, the treatment environment is the one that matters, and within that environment the experience is genuinely excellent.

What safety means in practice

Political and general safety

Sri Lanka resolved its 2022 economic crisis and returned to political stability. The FCDO does not advise against travel for most visitors. International tourism has recovered strongly. The country has low levels of violent crime and is a welcoming destination for international visitors.

Colombo's private hospitals operate to international standards with doctors trained in the UK, Australia, and the USA. The clinics we work with have 24-hour medical cover and clear emergency protocols. Transfer to a private hospital if required can typically be arranged within 30 to 60 minutes.

Common questions

Safety questions we are asked

What happened in Sri Lanka in 2022 and has it recovered?
Sri Lanka experienced a severe economic crisis in 2022, triggered by a combination of factors including foreign debt, the effects of the pandemic on tourism, and domestic policy decisions. Fuel shortages, power cuts, and medicine shortages affected daily life for Sri Lankans significantly. The government collapsed and a new administration was elected. With IMF support, the economy stabilised, shortages ended, and the country returned to normal function. International tourism, including medical tourism, recovered strongly through 2023 and 2024. The crisis is a relevant part of Sri Lanka's recent history, but it is not a current risk for visitors.
What happens if there is a medical emergency during treatment?
The facility has a 24-hour on-call medical team and clear emergency protocols. For situations that require hospital-level care, transfer to a private hospital in Colombo can typically be arranged within 30 to 60 minutes. Lanka Hospitals and Asiri Hospital operate to international private hospital standards. Your travel insurance, which we strongly recommend, should cover any inpatient treatment costs at a private hospital. The clinical team will accompany you and liaise with the hospital team.
Is it safe to travel to Sri Lanka as a solo traveller?
Yes. Sri Lanka is generally considered safe for solo international travellers. The country has low levels of violent crime. English is widely spoken in tourist and private healthcare settings. All clients are met at the airport on arrival and transferred directly to the facility, so you will not be navigating independently from the moment you land. The main practical safety consideration is having comprehensive travel insurance in place before you travel.
Does the FCDO advise against travel to Sri Lanka?
As of the most recent update, the FCDO advises normal travel precautions for most of Sri Lanka, which is the same advice given for many European countries. There are no specific warnings relevant to the areas where treatment facilities are located. We recommend checking the current FCDO advice at gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/sri-lanka before travel, as advice can change, and ensuring your travel insurer is aware of the destination and purpose of travel.
What should my travel insurance cover?
At minimum, your policy should cover medical emergency treatment, inpatient hospital costs, medical evacuation back to your home country if required, and trip cancellation or curtailment. For a treatment trip of 28 days or more, a specialist long-stay or medical travel insurance policy is often more appropriate than a standard holiday policy. Make sure you disclose the purpose of your travel (medical treatment) to your insurer. Most insurers will accommodate this; the key is full disclosure to avoid claims being rejected.
