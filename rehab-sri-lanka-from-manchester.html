Manchester residents and rehab abroad

Why Manchester residents are choosing Sri Lanka

Manchester is a city with a proud social culture. From the Northern Quarter's bars to the city's football-linked pub scene, alcohol is woven into the fabric of everyday Manchester life. For many people, that social context makes it genuinely difficult to recognise when use has crossed into dependency, and even harder to do something about it.

Greater Manchester has above-average alcohol consumption rates relative to the national average, and NHS addiction services in the region are under significant pressure. Waiting times for NHS-funded residential rehabilitation in Greater Manchester can stretch to 12 months or more, and eligibility criteria mean many people do not qualify even after that wait.

Private clinics in Greater Manchester and the surrounding area, including the Priory Manchester and other UKAT facilities, charge between £10,000 and £18,000 for a standard 28-day residential programme. That figure is beyond reach for many families, particularly when there is no clear end date on NHS provision.

Sri Lanka offers a credible, clinically sound alternative at a cost that works. Programmes typically run from $9,900 USD (approx. £7,800) for 28 days. Manchester Airport has good connections to Colombo via Dubai with Emirates, making the journey comfortable and manageable.

The case for going abroad

Why leaving Manchester is part of the treatment

For most people in residential treatment, getting distance from their home environment is not an optional extra. It is one of the most clinically important decisions they can make.

Manchester's drinking culture

Alcohol is central to Manchester social life in a way that makes local outpatient treatment extremely difficult. Pubs, bars, and social occasions built around drinking are unavoidable. Residential treatment abroad removes that exposure entirely during the critical early weeks of recovery.

Professional and social discretion

Manchester's professional networks are tight. Being seen at a local clinic, or taking unexplained leave, can feel exposing. Treatment in Sri Lanka means complete separation from colleagues, clients, and social connections during the programme.

A cost that is genuinely achievable

For many Manchester families, the cost of local private rehab is simply not realistic. Sri Lanka brings serious residential treatment within reach without requiring a second mortgage or years of savings. Including flights, the total cost is often lower than a single month at a Manchester clinic.

A restorative environment

Manchester's weather and urban pace are not conducive to recovery in the way that Sri Lanka's tropical climate and natural surroundings can be. The physical and psychological shift of being in a genuinely different environment has real therapeutic value.

‍‍

Family involvement from a distance

Many Manchester families worry that going abroad means losing touch with the process. In practice, clinics provide regular updates, family therapy sessions via video call, and structured family days for those who wish to visit. Distance does not mean disconnection.

Speed of admission

When someone is ready to act, waiting months defeats the purpose. Sri Lanka programmes can typically admit within one to two weeks of initial enquiry, which means readiness is not squandered by a system that cannot respond in time.

Getting there from Manchester

The journey from Manchester to Sri Lanka

There is no direct flight from Manchester to Colombo, but the connection via Dubai is comfortable and frequently available, making the journey straightforward for most clients.

1

Fly from Manchester Airport (MAN)

Manchester Airport is the largest airport outside London and has strong onward connections. The most popular route to Colombo is via Dubai with Emirates, which offers multiple daily departures from Manchester. Qatar Airways via Doha is also a good option.

2

Connect via Dubai or Doha

Emirates connects Manchester to Colombo (CMB) via Dubai (DXB) with a typical total journey time of 13 to 14 hours. Qatar Airways connects via Doha in a similar timeframe. Both hubs have excellent transit facilities and short layovers are the norm.

3

Arrival in Colombo

All arrivals at Bandaranaike International Airport are met and transferred directly to the treatment facility. Clients do not need to navigate Sri Lanka independently. The transfer is arranged as part of the admission process.

Common questions from Manchester clients

Questions we hear from Manchester

Is Sri Lanka safe for someone travelling for treatment?
Yes. Sri Lanka is a stable, welcoming country with well-established private healthcare infrastructure. The clinics we work with have been receiving international clients for many years and have clear protocols for airport transfers, medical management, and client welfare. The experience for the vast majority of clients is smooth from the moment they land.
How do NHS services in Greater Manchester compare?
NHS addiction services in Greater Manchester are primarily community-based. Residential treatment is available through the NHS but is means-tested, criteria-dependent, and subject to significant waiting times. Many people who would benefit from residential care do not meet the threshold for NHS-funded provision, or face waits of 12 months or more. Sri Lanka offers admission within days for those who are ready to commit to treatment.
Does private health insurance cover treatment in Sri Lanka?
Most UK private health insurance policies have limited or no cover for overseas residential addiction treatment. Bupa, AXA, and similar providers typically require pre-authorisation and may not recognise overseas facilities. We recommend checking your specific policy. In practice, most Manchester clients fund treatment privately, and Sri Lanka's lower costs make this substantially more achievable than a UK clinic.
What if someone is physically dependent and needs a medically supervised detox?
The clinics we work with have medically supervised detoxification as part of their residential programmes. A detailed pre-admission assessment is completed before travel to determine medical needs. Where required, medication-assisted detox is provided by qualified medical staff on arrival. This is assessed individually and is not an obstacle to travelling for treatment.
How does aftercare work when I return to Manchester?
A structured aftercare and relapse prevention plan is prepared before discharge. This typically includes recommendations for local support in Greater Manchester, including NA and AA meetings, SMART Recovery groups, and private therapy referrals if required. The transition back to Manchester is planned, not left to chance.
