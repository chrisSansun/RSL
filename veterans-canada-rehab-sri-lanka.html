Veterans Affairs Canada

What Canadian veterans need to know about VAC and overseas treatment

Veterans Affairs Canada (VAC) provides a range of health and rehabilitation benefits for eligible Canadian Armed Forces veterans. For veterans dealing with mental health conditions and addiction, the most relevant programs are the Rehabilitation Services and Vocational Assistance Program (RSVAP) and the Mental Health Program, which funds treatment for PTSD, depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders linked to service.

The VAC Rehabilitation Program provides case-managed access to medical rehabilitation, psychosocial rehabilitation, and vocational rehabilitation for veterans whose conditions are related to their service. Where mental health treatment is required, VAC can authorise funding for approved providers - including, in some cases, residential treatment programs outside Canada where clinically justified and pre-approved.

Canadian veterans also have access to the Operational Stress Injury (OSI) Clinics - a network of specialist mental health clinics funded by VAC across Canada that provide assessment and outpatient therapy for PTSD and other operational stress injuries. OSI Clinics are a valuable resource for outpatient support, but do not provide residential addiction treatment. For veterans who need intensive residential care, private treatment remains the most accessible route.

Canada is well-connected to Sri Lanka. From Toronto, a connecting flight via a Gulf or Asian hub takes approximately 17 to 20 hours. From Vancouver, connecting through Seoul, Tokyo, or Singapore, the journey is approximately 16 to 18 hours. Private residential treatment in Sri Lanka starts from $9,900 USD per month - significantly below the cost of comparable Canadian private residential care, which ranges from CAD $25,000 to CAD $50,000 for 28 days.

Why Canadian veterans choose Sri Lanka

What overseas private treatment offers Canadian veterans

Canada has a strong network of veteran mental health services, but residential addiction treatment with integrated PTSD care remains difficult to access quickly. Here is what Sri Lanka provides.

Integrated PTSD and addiction treatment

OSI Clinics and VAC-funded outpatient services treat PTSD effectively, but the residential, integrated treatment of co-occurring PTSD and addiction in a single programme remains limited in Canada. Sri Lanka provides exactly this - trauma and addiction treated concurrently, residentially, by a specialist team.

Faster than VAC approval timelines

Navigating VAC rehabilitation approvals, waiting for OSI Clinic assessments, and finding a residential bed can take weeks to months. For veterans in crisis or ready to act, private overseas admission within 5 to 10 days removes the wait entirely.

Cost below Canadian private care

Private residential addiction treatment in Canada costs between CAD $25,000 and CAD $50,000 for 28 days. Sri Lanka starts from $9,900 USD (approximately CAD $13,500) per month - making a 60 to 90-day programme achievable at the cost of a single month's domestic private care.

Privacy outside the VAC system

Private overseas treatment operates completely outside the VAC system. Records are not shared with the Canadian Armed Forces or VAC without your explicit written consent. Many Canadian veterans, particularly those with active service or security clearances, choose private overseas treatment specifically for this reason.

Complete separation from Canadian life

Geographic distance from the communities, networks, and triggers of home is consistently reported as one of the most clinically significant factors in veterans' recovery. Sri Lanka's climate, environment, and complete removal from Canadian daily life provide a genuine therapeutic break.

Accessible from both coasts

From Toronto, the journey to Colombo via a single hub takes 17 to 20 hours. From Vancouver, via Seoul, Singapore, or Tokyo, it is approximately 16 to 18 hours. Emirates, Qatar Airways, Singapore Airlines, and Air Canada codeshares all serve this route with comfortable connections.

Common questions

Questions from Canadian veterans and their families

Will VAC fund residential treatment in Sri Lanka?
VAC can fund overseas treatment where it is clinically justified and pre-approved through the Rehabilitation Services Program. The key requirements are that the veteran has an eligible condition related to service, that residential treatment is clinically indicated, and that the overseas provider is a qualified facility. We can prepare documentation to support a VAC application for overseas treatment. For veterans who prefer to move quickly, self-funded treatment is available without VAC approval.
I am already engaged with an OSI Clinic. Would going to Sri Lanka affect that?
No. Private overseas residential treatment operates independently of your OSI Clinic engagement. Records are not shared between private providers and the OSI Clinic without your consent. Many veterans use private residential treatment as an intensive phase of care and then return to OSI Clinic outpatient support as continuing care. We encourage this model and can communicate with your OSI Clinic on your behalf, with your permission, to support seamless transition back to Canadian care.
Will treatment in Sri Lanka affect my VAC disability award or pension?
No. Private overseas treatment records are not automatically submitted to VAC. Your entitlements are not affected by accessing private treatment. Treatment records can, with your explicit consent, be submitted as supporting evidence in VAC claims or reviews - many veterans find that a formal clinical assessment and treatment record from an international provider strengthens a claim that has previously been denied or underrated.
I served in Afghanistan. Do I qualify for VAC rehabilitation benefits?
Service in Afghanistan is recognised qualifying service under the Veterans Well-being Act. Veterans with conditions - including PTSD, depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders - that are related to service in Afghanistan may be eligible for VAC rehabilitation and health benefits. Contact VAC on 1-866-522-2122 to confirm your eligibility based on your specific service record and conditions.
What is the time difference between Canada and Sri Lanka?
Sri Lanka Standard Time (SLST) is UTC+5:30. From Eastern Time (ET), Sri Lanka is 10.5 hours ahead in winter (EST) and 9.5 hours ahead in summer (EDT). From Pacific Time, add 3 hours. This means evening calls from Canadian family members fall during Sri Lanka daytime hours, making family contact straightforward throughout treatment.
What support is available when I return to Canada?
Aftercare planning is built into the treatment programme. On return to Canada, this typically includes re-engagement with VAC and an OSI Clinic for continuing mental health support, referral to a community addiction service if ongoing structured support is needed, and a relapse prevention plan developed during residential care. VAC's Transition Services team can also assist with re-integration support for veterans returning from an extended period away.
